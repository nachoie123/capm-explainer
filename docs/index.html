CAPM Explainer

Type a company name or ticker and watch its expected return get derived, one animated step at a time: where the risk-free rate comes from, how beta is measured, what the market premium is. Not a number, a derivation.

One formula, taken apart

E[R] = Rf + β · (E[Rm] − Rf)

The expected return of a stock is what you earn for lending to the safe borrower (Rf), plus extra for the risk you can't diversify away (β times the market premium). The app computes each piece from real data instead of asking you to type it in.

2.50% + 1.141 × 2.93% = 5.84%. Beta comes from a regression of 59 monthly returns against the Euro Stoxx 50 (R² = 45%). The market return, 5.43%, comes from the Gordon model: dividend yield 2.36% × (1 + 3%) + 3%. Numbers are the app's real output, recorded in the video above.

How each number is found

Beta by regression

Covariance of the stock with its market index over variance of the index, on monthly returns. It shows R² so you can see how much to trust it.

Risk-free rate

US stocks use the live 10-year Treasury (^TNX). EUR and GBP use an adjustable default, because Yahoo has no European bond yields.

Forward market return

The Gordon model: dividend yield × (1 + g) + g, not a historical average. The growth g is yours to change.

Search by name

"Repsol" resolves to REP.MC through Yahoo's search. US, Eurozone and UK markets are supported.

Honest warnings

Repsol's beta comes out at 0.02 with R² of 0.02%: Yahoo's data for it is noisy. The app says so instead of showing a fake beta.

Look at the US rows: the forward market return from the Gordon model (5.02%) is currently below the 10-year yield (5.28%), so the premium is negative and a higher beta gives a lower expected return. That is the model being honest about a thin dividend yield, and it is why the app lets you change g. A known limitation, written in the README.

Install on your Mac

About a minute. Step 3 happens only the first time.

  1. Drag it into Applications

    Open the .dmg and drag CAPM Explainer onto the Applications folder.

    CAPM Explainer
    Applications
  2. Open it. macOS stops it. Click Done.

    macOS says Apple could not verify “CAPM Explainer” is free of malware. It says that about every app that isn't notarized by Apple. Click Done, not Move to Trash.

    “CAPM Explainer” Not Opened

    Apple could not verify “CAPM Explainer” is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy.

    DoneMove to Trash
  3. Open Anyway, once

    Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to Security and click Open Anyway next to “CAPM Explainer” was blocked. Confirm with your password or Touch ID. From then on it opens with a double-click.

    Privacy & Security
    Security
    “CAPM Explainer” was blocked to protect your Mac.Open Anyway
  4. Then: type a company

    Write a name or ticker (Inditex, KO, Apple) and press Calcular. The app speaks Spanish and needs internet for Yahoo Finance data.

Why the warning? Notarizing an app needs a paid Apple developer account ($99 a year). This is a free side project, so it isn't notarized. The full source code is on GitHub, and you can check that your download is exactly the file I built. In Terminal:

shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/CAPM-Explainer-AppleSilicon.dmg

(Apple Silicon) should print 6eeddb8bc0a542db4186127caedd61975e0ee458bb6b54591ac1b9b023145f1c

shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/CAPM-Explainer-Intel.dmg

(Intel) should print aef94da3e8a374c3998397a4e8fdfcad92a31ea27f70417cdcad78f5f3c2b0fb

Prefer the Terminal for step 3?
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/CAPM Explainer.app"

Or run it from source

git clone https://github.com/nachoie123/capm-explainer
cd capm-explainer
pip install -r requirements.txt
python server.py   # then open http://localhost:8000

The app needs its small Python server (it fetches Yahoo data and does the maths), so it can't run as a static page here. There's also a command line: python capm.py KO.
