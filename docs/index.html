CAPM Explainer Where an expected return comes from.

Type a company. The app fits beta to five years of real monthly returns, then builds the expected return piece by piece.

The app, in 40 seconds.

Inditex, from ticker to 5.84%: every step the app shows, with voice and music.

Beta is the slope that misses the least.

Each square is one month's miss: the gap from the dot to the line, squared. Least squares (OLS) picks the slope with the smallest total area. Move the slider and try to beat it.

The app does it in one line: β = Cov(Ri, Rm) / Var(Rm), on monthly returns from Yahoo Finance, against the index of the stock's region.

The other two pieces.

Beta says how much market risk you carry. These two say what that risk pays.

  1. Rf = 2.50%

    The safe rate. US stocks use the live 10-year Treasury (^TNX, 5.28% in the runs on this page). Yahoo has no Bund yield, so euro and pound stocks start from a default you can change.

  2. E[Rm] = 2.36% × (1 + 3%) + 3% = 5.43%

    The market's return, looking forward, by the Gordon model: the dividend yield of a Euro Stoxx 50 ETF (EXW1.DE), grown at g = 3%. Not a historical average. g is yours to change.

  3. Premium = 5.43% − 2.50% = 2.93%

    What the market pays above the safe rate.

  4. E[R] = 2.50% + 1.141 × 2.93% = 5.84%

    What an investor should ask of Inditex per year, for the market risk it carries. The app's real output for ITX.MC.

Where it breaks.

A number with no error bar is a guess. All of these are measured on the same data.

44.5%R², Inditex
of Inditex's monthly moves are explained by the market. The other 55.5% is the company's own news, which CAPM says you are not paid for. The app prints R² next to every beta.
0.80 to 1.4895% range for β
Standard error 0.169 over 59 months. Push both ends through the formula and the 5.84% becomes anything from 4.85% to 6.84%. The point estimate is the middle of a wide band.
1.13 → 1.18β, first half → second half
Beta over the first 29 months (Dec 2021 to Apr 2024) against the last 30 (May 2024 to Oct 2026). It holds steady, so the slope is not a fluke of one period.
−0.26%US market premium

The Gordon return of the S&P 500 (5.02%: a thin 0.98% dividend yield grown at g = 4%) sits below the 10-year Treasury (5.28%). The premium turns negative, so in the US a riskier stock gets a lower expected return. That is the model being honest about its inputs, and why g is adjustable.

Install on your Mac

About a minute. Step 3 happens only the first time.

  1. Drag it into Applications

    Open the .dmg and drag CAPM Explainer onto the Applications folder.

    CAPM Explainer
    Applications
  2. Open it. macOS stops it. Click Done.

    macOS says Apple could not verify “CAPM Explainer” is free of malware. It says that about every app that isn't notarized by Apple. Click Done, not Move to Trash.

    “CAPM Explainer” Not Opened

    Apple could not verify “CAPM Explainer” is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy.

    DoneMove to Trash
  3. Open Anyway, once

    Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to Security and click Open Anyway next to “CAPM Explainer” was blocked. Confirm with your password or Touch ID. From then on it opens with a double-click.

    Privacy & Security
    Security
    “CAPM Explainer” was blocked to protect your Mac.Open Anyway
  4. Then: type a company

    Write a name or ticker (Inditex, KO, Apple) and press Calcular. The app speaks Spanish and needs internet for Yahoo Finance data.

Why the warning? Notarizing an app needs a paid Apple developer account ($99 a year). This is a free side project, so it isn't notarized. The full source code is on GitHub, and you can check that your download is exactly the file I built. In Terminal:

shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/CAPM-Explainer-AppleSilicon.dmg

(Apple Silicon) should print 6eeddb8bc0a542db4186127caedd61975e0ee458bb6b54591ac1b9b023145f1c

shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/CAPM-Explainer-Intel.dmg

(Intel) should print aef94da3e8a374c3998397a4e8fdfcad92a31ea27f70417cdcad78f5f3c2b0fb

Prefer the Terminal for step 3?
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/CAPM Explainer.app"

Or run it from source

git clone https://github.com/nachoie123/capm-explainer
cd capm-explainer
pip install -r requirements.txt
python server.py   # then open http://localhost:8000

The app needs its small Python server (it fetches Yahoo data and does the maths), so it can't run as a static page here. There's also a command line: python capm.py KO.
